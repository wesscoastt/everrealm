# Serra: the third continent (story draft)

A draft for the owner to read before anything is built. Everything here is **[planned]**. The rules in
LORE.md and NARRATIVE_STYLE.md hold: plain names, plain speech, no prophecy, and the trouble always has
a human cause. Where this draft and LORE.md disagree, LORE.md wins and this changes.

## The land

- **Serra** lies south of the Counties and Morrow, three days' sail on the packet from Callis, longer
  from Gullmouth. Its own people call it Serra. Counties sailors call it **Sorrow**, because before the
  packet ran the ships that went south mostly didn't come back, and because it rhymes with Morrow.
- About **35% larger than Morrow** (roughly eight kilometres north to south and five across), with a
  ring of islands off it, each with its own people. The **Debatable Land**, the war zone, is attached to
  its north coast and comes on top of that size.
- **The green.** The Counties have the white crystal (it lifts) and Morrow has the black (it holds
  things down). Serra has a third: the green, which makes things grow. Where it lies near the surface
  the woods grow huge and never quite stop, fruit sets twice a year, and people who live on it all their
  lives live a long time. Serra's history is a history of who owns the ground over the green.
- **The Hill.** The great city, Ambry, stands on a hill in the middle of the land. The hill is warm in
  winter. Its springs come up hot. Three hundred years ago the cathedral builders dug its footings and
  found scale. The Hill is a dragon, asleep, and the city has known it for three hundred years and
  decided not to talk about it.

### The people

- **Serrans** of the north and the Hill Country: guildsfolk, farmers and townspeople, a culture modelled
  on Europe around 1400 (Florence, Bruges, Prague): banks that lend to kings, guilds that run the
  towns, a cathedral that's been building for a hundred and forty years and isn't finished, a
  university, plague in living memory. Names are short and Italian- or Flemish-sounding: Pella, Corvo,
  Bassa, Mattei, Hoek, Ruys, Vos.
- **The Sallows** are the forest people, the elf-like folk the owner asked for: taller than Serrans,
  long-lived (a Sallow of a hundred is in middle age), quiet, and very hard to lie to. They're not
  magical. They have lived their whole lives on top of the green and eaten what grows on it, and it
  shows. Their towns are built into and between the great trees. They keep the oldest records on the
  continent, carved into living wood, and the wood keeps growing round the words. Outsiders named them
  for the sallow-willows; they don't mind. Names are single and plain, one or two syllables: Ash, Wend,
  Tamsel, Rook, Fen. They keep their word and expect you to keep yours.
- **The Cane Coast** folk: free farmers, plantation hands, fishers and tar-boilers, many descended
  from people the Ambry banks shipped south to cut cane two hundred years ago. Hard-working, funny,
  suspicious of anyone in a good coat. Names from everywhere.

### The islands

Each is small (a few hundred blocks across), a day trip on the island packet, with its own people,
look, music and a quest chain.

| Island | Who lives there | Its story |
| --- | --- | --- |

| **Murra** | Glassblowers: the whole island is furnaces, the Ambry guild's secret | Leaving is against guild law. A glassblower's daughter wants out, and the guild wants her back |
| **The Bell Rocks** | Lighthouse keepers, a family trade for twelve generations | Somebody has been showing false lights to wreck the Cane Coast ships |
| **Salt Holm** | Salt-makers, run by the grandmothers, who own everything and say so | The young men want to sell the pans to an Ambry bank; the grandmothers don't |
| **Corrie** | Whalers on a cold, high island in the south-west, turf houses, sheep | The whales haven't come this year, and the whalers have started on the Sallows' river-dolphins |
| **The Brother's Isle** | Monks who keep bees and make mead, and say very little | One monk was a famous Ambry banker; the bank wants its money and its secrets back |
| **Tor Ama** | Pearl divers on a ring of reef round a sunk volcano | A diver came up with a pearl the size of a fist and the island's been at war with itself ever since |

## Ambry, the great city

The European city of about 1400 the owner asked for, and the largest place in the game: an hour to walk
round properly, four or five times the size of Callis.

- **The walls and gates**: three rings of wall from three centuries, the old ones built over or knocked
  through. Gatehouses with tolls, a moat on the north side.
- **The Hill** in the middle, the cathedral of **Saint Ilse** on its top: a hundred and forty years
  building, the dome still open to the sky and covered with a wooden roof every winter. The builders'
  yard is a district of its own.
- **The guild districts**: dyers along the river (the water runs blue, red or yellow by the day of the
  week), weavers, goldsmiths on the bridge, glass (the Murra guild's shop), the butchers' Shambles, the
  printers who aren't allowed to exist yet.
- **The banks**: the **Corvo** and the **Bassa**, two families whose palaces face each other across the
  Piazza Nova, who lend to the Charter, to the Assembly and to each other. The Corvo bank's counting
  house is the city's tallest building after the cathedral, on purpose.
- **The university** with its anatomy theatre, its library and its students who drink in the Ox and
  argue about whether the Hill is breathing.
- **The baths**: hot springs piped up from under the Hill, public and private, the city's pride and its
  gossip-market.
- **The port**, down the river at Ambry Mouth: galleys, the customs house, the Lazaret on the horizon.

## The main story: The Hill

Eight chapters, from Act IV on: the third act of the Everrealm's story, after the Third Daughter.
It begins with a letter from Delia Harrowgate and ends with a city choosing what to do with a dragon.

1. **A Letter of Credit.** Harrowgate & Daughters has borrowed from the Corvo bank of Ambry to rebuild
   after the Third Daughter, and the bank has called the loan early, without warning. Delia sends you
   south with the books to find out why. At landfall on the Shingle Coast, the Corvo agent has gone and
   so has every other bank's: they've all been called back to Ambry at once.
2. **Warm Ground.** Ambry's springs have been running hotter for a year. The cathedral's crypt is too
   hot to stand in. The Corvo have been buying every forge and furnace in the Hill Country and running
   them on hot water piped from under the Hill, cheaper than any coal, and calling in loans to pay for
   the pipes. They're drawing heat out of the dragon.
3. **What the Wood Remembers.** The Sallows have the oldest record of the Hill, carved in a living oak:
   the dragon lay down to sleep on the green when the land was young, and the green keeps it asleep.
   The Corvo pipes go down to the green seam under the Hill. To reach the wood you need a Sallow to
   speak for you, and Sallows don't speak for liars. (The companion **Wend**, a Sallow, joins here.)
4. **Cane and Pitch.** The Corvo pay for the pipes with sugar. On the Cane Coast, the plantation hands
   have stopped cutting: the ground has gone warm there too, and the cane is growing faster than they
   can cut it. The shanty towns over the Pitch are the only place the Corvo's enemies, the Bassa, can
   meet you without being seen. They want you to sabotage the pipes. They also own half the cane.
5. **The Turning.** The Hill turns over in its sleep. A quarter of the old town slides; the cathedral's
   wooden roof comes down; the dome's open now and the city looks up through it at a scaled side the
   size of a church. Ambry has known for three hundred years and has never once said "dragon" out loud.
   It does now.
6. **Three Ways.** The city has three answers, and each has a people behind it:
   - the **Corvo**: wake it and kill it, while it's slow, and sell the bones and the heat for a hundred years;
   - the **Sallows**: put the green back and let it sleep, which means tearing out every pipe and
     ruining the families that paid for them, the Corvo and the guilds and half the Hill Country;
   - the **Bassa**: move it. The green runs under the whole continent; draw it south along the seams to
     the Burnt Reach where nobody lives, and let it sleep there, and own the ground it leaves.
7. **Under the Hill.** Down through the crypt, the pipes and the green seam to the dragon itself: the
   game's first ten-player raid (the trial the owner approved in the ideas backlog). The dragon is the
   continent's centrepiece: **the Old Mother of Serra**, the Glassmother's own mother, as big as the
   cathedral, and what you do down there depends on which way the city chose.
8. **The Ambry Accord.** The ending. As in every act, the choice changes the world: Ambry rebuilt over a
   dead dragon (rich, ugly, quarrelling over the bones); Ambry beside a sleeping one with its pipes torn
   out and its banks broke; or Ambry on cold ground and a dragon asleep in the south, and the Bassa
   the richest family in the world. Delia's loan is settled in every ending, but on very different terms.

The **Glassmother** (Morrow's world boss) is the Old Mother's daughter. Players who've fought her will
hear it said, and the Sallows will have something to say about the Glass Flats.

## Side activities

- **The guilds of Ambry** (a standing track like the Brethren's): join one of five (dyers, goldsmiths,
  glass, builders, printers), work up through apprentice, journeyman and master, and unlock its
  crafted sets and its say in the city.
- **The banks**: invest in a voyage (marks in, marks out a week later, sometimes); ruin or rescue a
  merchant house; the Corvo and Bassa standing tracks pull against each other.
- **Cane Coast smuggling**: run sugar and tar past the customs galleys in your own ship.
- **Pitch diving**: the Pitch gives back what it swallowed a century ago; dive for it.
- **The cathedral builders**: weekly building tasks that visibly raise the dome over the expansion's life.
- **Sallow trials**: the forest's challenges (keep your word, find the oldest tree, run the canopy road).
- **Island festivals**, each island with its own day in the calendar.

## The Debatable Land (the war zone)

The Cyrodiil-style PvP region the owner asked for, attached to Serra's north coast, on top of its size.

- **Three sides**: the **Charter** (the Counties), the **Assembly** (Morrow), and the **Ambry League**
  (Serra's cities). A character picks a side for the length of a campaign.
- **Keeps and outposts** to siege and hold: six keeps (walls, gates, a lord to kill inside), ten
  outposts, three towns. Siege weapons built from resources.
- **Resource farms, mills and mines** round each keep: holding them speeds your side's repairs and siege.
- **The three Seals**: one relic per side, kept deep in its home keep. Steal another side's Seal and
  carry it home for the campaign's biggest bonus.
- **Campaign score**: holding keeps, outposts and Seals scores by the hour; a campaign runs two weeks
  and the winners' rewards are cosmetic and titles, never power (the store rule holds here too).
- Players who don't want PvP never have to go in; Serra's story doesn't send you there.

## Dungeons, delves and bosses

- **Dungeons (five-player)**: the Corvo Counting House (its vaults go down into the Hill); the
  Drowned Abbey on the Lazaret; the Pitch's Bottom; the Burnt Reach's Bone Gallery; the Sallow
  King's Root.
- **Delves (solo)**: a dozen across the land and islands, one per region at least.
- **Trial (ten-player)**: Under the Hill, the Old Mother of Serra.
- **World bosses**: a tar-giant that crawls out of the Pitch; a Sallow Wood guardian gone wrong; a
  lava wyrm in the Burnt Reach; an old white whale off Corrie that has sunk eleven whaleboats.

## New creatures

Cane-snakes, mud-crabs the size of carts, tar-wights (what the Pitch gives back), jungle cats, the
Sallow Wood's moss-stags and owl-bears, canopy monkeys that steal, Shelves goats and cave bats, lava
lizards and fire beetles in the Burnt Reach, the Old Mother's brood of young dragons, and on the
islands: sea lions, rays, reef sharks and the whales.

## Music

A new suite for Serra in the existing orchestra: a lute-and-recorder **Ambry** theme in a Renaissance
dance style; low flutes, harp and choir for the **Sallow Wood**; marimba and
hand drums for the **Cane Coast**; a slow, huge brass-and-choir theme for **the Hill** that grows across
the story; war drums for the Debatable Land.

## Building order (when the owner says go)

1. Terrain and regions for Serra (south of both continents, in the same world), the packet south, the
   island packet. Same order as Morrow: land, then cities, then people.
2. Ambry, district by district (the biggest site in the game; impostors per district), then the
   Shingle Coast towns, the Sallow tree-towns, Pitch Landing and the cave towns.
3. Creatures, hazards (the Pitch, the vents, the Hill's hot ground), the islands one at a time.
4. The Hill, chapter by chapter, with Wend as a companion.
5. The guilds and banks standing, the side activities.
6. The Debatable Land last: it needs the realm server's three-sided war and the siege systems.
