# Everrealm: the main story, in summary

A summary of Everrealm's main story to paste into another assistant. The full plan is docs/STORY_PLAN.md, and
docs/LORE.md is the canon.

## The game

Everrealm is a cozy and adventurous browser MMO with a voxel look. It aims at the best of ESO, Guild Wars 2 and
WoW, with no pay-to-win. Its world is grounded medieval fantasy:
- trade charters, debts and toll roads;
- harbours and mines;
- mostly human trouble, with a little strangeness in the ground.

There is no chosen one, no world-ending prophecy and no dark lord. Every trouble in it has a human cause.

## The setting

**The Charter Counties.** Six regions are bound to the city of Ansbury by trade charters, not by conquest:
- Hollindale, around Hollin Ford, where you start;
- the Turnpike, around Ansbury;
- Hagworm Wood;
- the Fleet Coast, with the port of Gullmouth;
- the Raddle Barrens, with Harrowgate Works;
- the Scarth Highlands.

The coin is the mark. The Charter Post runs carts and ferries between the towns.

**Morrow.** A second continent over the sea:
- Callis, a tall city of towers, governed by the Assembly;
- Cold Harbour;
- the Greywaste;
- Northgate's wall, which holds back the Held.

**Serra.** A planned third continent, a southern land of many island cultures and the Hill.

**Verandah.** A wealthy port city and its countryside, imported block for block from the owner's own Minecraft world.

## The spine

Every act has the same shape:
- a debt;
- someone who bought it cheap;
- ground that lifts, sinks or gets up when something is taken out of it.

**The Harrowgate sisters**, of Harrowgate & Daughters, the company that bids on every contract the Charter puts out:
- **Hilda** wants the debt paid and thinks she's the only adult in the room. Over the story she goes from a hard woman
  you can argue with to someone who will drain a living thing for its heat and call it sense.
- **Joan** would rather be right than loyal. She dies stopping Hilda.
- **Delia** signs whatever she's given without reading it. In the end she reads the books and has to choose between
  her sisters.

**Aubrey Pryce** bought every debt in the story at three marks in ten. He ends up the only honest man in a room of
liars.

**Books of names** recur throughout:
- the Book of Names at the Glass Kirk;
- Holmsey's parish register;
- the counting stones of the Forty-One;
- Pryce's ledger;
- the Gatekeepers' rolls.

Each act turns on someone writing names down, or refusing to. The last chapter asks you whose name goes first.

**The player** is not chosen and not special. You are a useful stranger with a strong arm who keeps being in the room
when things go wrong, and whose friends come to matter more than the debt.

## The companions

They travel with you, have opinions of what you do (rapport), and disagree with each other. Each has a camp scene at
the end of every act.

| Companion | Joins | Who they are | What they want | What they hide | Where their story turns |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wenna Hale** | Act I | Once a Toll Rider; she tanks | For the Charter to deserve the oath she swore to it | She left the Riders because she was told to look away, and once she did | She meets an aunt she never knew, and loses her nine days later |
| **Ottilie Fenn** | Act I | A surveyor's apprentice; she heals | To measure something nobody has measured | She fears she is only clever, not good | She becomes Joan's apprentice, and after Joan's death ends the game as the Charter's surveyor |

## The losses

Each loss is set up an act or more before it lands, and none happens only to make you sad.
- Ursel Hale, Wenna's aunt, holds a postern alone and dies.
- The Old Tower of Callis falls.
- In that fall you can reach Wenna or Tam, not both. There is a third way, if you've earned their trust, and it
  costs something else instead.
- Rhosyn Tarrant, the Charter's Speaker and your guide, turns out to have signed the papers behind the disaster.
- Joan Harrowgate dies.
- The herring port of Stair burns.
- Wat Aske dies if you let him go alone.

## The wrong turns

In each of these you do the reasonable thing and it goes badly.
- You burn the ship Kittiwake on a smuggler's word, and she was carrying refugee families.
- You lay the "black" (a stone the dead are bound with) back in the barrows. That quiets the dead, but you took it
  from the stones holding up Callis's Old Tower.
- You help chain the Old Mother (a great dragon) to the Hill, and the chains turn out to be the pipes draining her.

## The acts

The plan is about 112 chapters in ten acts, 60 to 80 hours. Acts I to IV and the first half of Act V are built.

**Part one: the Counties**
- **Act I, The Charter's Debt.** From a notice in Hollin Ford, through the Turnpike, the Glass Kirk where the
  floor is rising where Harrowgate bored, the Fallen Star, and the Lift, to the Charter's sitting. Wenna and Ottilie
  join.
- **Act II, The Weatherholms.** Islands whose ground sinks a stride a week. Ilse's logbooks and the forty-one
  islanders' count. A rope down from the isle.
- **Act III, The Drowned Parish.** A parish going under the sea, its register, and the last names. Tam joins.

**Part two: Morrow**
- **Act IV, The Third Daughter.** Over the sea to Morrow:
  - the Kittiwake burned on a smuggler's word;
  - Tam's letters to Pryce found out;
  - Wenna meets her aunt Ursel at Northgate;
  - the first page of Pryce's book is signed "R. Tarrant, for the Charter".
- **Act V, The Held.** The dead in the barrows are getting up and walking south:
  - escorting Cold Harbour's families;
  - the Gatekeepers' rolls;
  - Ursel's death at the postern;
  - the siege of Northgate, a public event;
  - a second "A.P." signing in Pryce's name.
- **Act VI, The Tall City.** Callis without its black:
  - the Assembly's towers and bond office;
  - the Speaker's secret;
  - the Old Tower falls;
  - the Charter sits abroad for the first time;
  - Delia reads the books.

**Part three: Serra**
- **Act VII, Landfall.** Stair burns as the Old Mother passes over. Wend gets you out. The Sallow wood, the Corvo
  family's furnaces, and the cathedral wardens' chains.
- **Act VIII, The Green.** Hilda comes south, buying the Hill's debt. Joan follows her into the pipes to shut the
  green seam, and her last reading comes up the speaking tube. Delia chooses a sister.
- **Act IX, The Hill.** Three ways forward (the Corvo, the Sallows, the Bassa), a city vote, a raid under the Hill,
  Hilda's last cut, and an ending shaped by everything you chose.

**Part four: home**
- **Act X, The Book of Names.** The packet ship home; the Ford Bell rings you into Hollin Ford; what became of
  everyone; Pryce's last line; and at the Glass Kirk, whose name goes first.

## Regional stories alongside it, ESO-style

Each region has its own complete story beside the main one:
- **Verandah's "The Silent Waters":** merchant ships and fishing boats going missing, smuggling, and corruption
  among powerful merchant houses (planned).
- **The Fisherman Dave and Becky White questline** (Dave is in the game; his story is being rewritten to the owner's brief): a ghost in town, a grieving drunk fisherman, a strange rancher
  father, a magical Pilgrim Hat, and a reaper-like Cursekeeper whose death frees Becky (planned).
- **Group dungeons and public dungeons** with their own small stories, such as Black Gull Keep's wreckers and the
  Sunken Temple's drowned prior.

## The writing

BioWare and CD Projekt Red in tone:
- plain speech;
- no prophecy;
- no generic fantasy talk;
- no AI-sounding names or phrasing;
- characters who want things and won't admit things;
- humour that comes from who people are;
- choices that come back later.
