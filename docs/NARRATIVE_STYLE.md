# Everrealm narrative style guide

The full standard is the narrative brief. This page is the working version that writers check against.

## Rules that catch most problems

1. **Specific beats grand.** A cracked bell, a rent increase and a rising rain gauge are a story. "A darkness stirs" is not.
2. **People talk about their own problems first.** The player is a useful stranger, not the centre of anyone's life.
3. **No speeches.** Most lines run under 25 words. If a line needs to be longer, the speaker should have a reason to go on: they're nervous, lying, or showing off.
4. **Plain words for plain things.** The smith says "bars", not "ingots of destiny".
5. **Subtext over confession.** A guilty person changes the subject, gets over-technical, or makes a joke. They don't announce their guilt.
6. **No greeting boilerplate.** No "Welcome, adventurer", "Greetings, traveller", or "Ah, it's you again."
7. **Choices are between reasonable people.** Every option in a decision has a cost and someone who would defend it.
8. **Only promise what the game does.** If an outcome isn't tracked by a flag and visible somewhere, the dialogue can't hint at it.
9. **System text is short and literal.** Use "Mine copper ore (2/3)", not "Delve into the depths for precious copper".
10. **Item text fits the item.** Tools get practical notes, materials get one useful fact, and only items with real history get history.

## Banned on sight

Destiny or chosen-one framing, "ancient evil", "only time will tell", "more than meets the eye", "we meet again", "your journey begins", "the fate of the world", trailer-voice narration, a three-item list in every description, rhetorical-question openers, and ellipses as a stand-in for emotion.

The owner's standing rule is no AI tropes: it should read as authentic. So these are out too: embark, delve, tapestry, whispers, echoes, prophecy framing, "...or something more?", purple prose, and cryptic one-line warnings from people who should just say what they mean. A line that sounds like a riddle gets rewritten as plain speech.

## Names

Every name in the game, items included, has to be its own and not the kind AI writing reaches for. No made-up compounds like Stormreach, Ironhold or Shadowfall, and no Elara, Kael, Lyra or Thorne. Follow the vale instead: surnames from trades and places (Tolley, Garrow, Pargeter, Ashby) and places named for what's there (Gullmouth, the Spill, Sallow Pit, the Low Water). Still to rename: the Great Starfall, Cinderhorn Pit, Tidewright Sands and the Ashcaller calling.

## Review passes for each batch

Run these on every batch: specificity, voice, read-aloud, purpose, gameplay truth. A line that fails one of them gets cut or rewritten before it goes into `src/data/`.
