# Morrow: the second continent (plan)

Everything here is **[planned]** unless marked otherwise. The crossing, Callis, Saltpenny Key, Morrow's creatures and chapters XVII to XIX are in the game now (LORE.md, "Morrow, the second continent"). When a piece is built, move
it into LORE.md and mark it [in game]. The rules in LORE.md and NARRATIVE_STYLE.md hold here too:
plain names, plain speech, no world-ending prophecy, and the trouble always has a human cause.

## The land

- **Morrow** lies east over the open sea from Gullmouth, about a day and a night's sail. Counties
  sailors call it "the other side", and say you'll get there tomorrow. Same size as the first
  continent (about six kilometres end to end), but a different shape: long from north to south,
  with a mountain spine down the west side, rivers running east to the sea, and a long tail of
  low islands, the Keys, curling away south and west.
- **The white and the black.** The Counties' star crystal is the white, and it lifts. Morrow has
  the white too, and it also has the black: dense crystal that holds things down. Morrow's old
  builders used both. They raised their cities impossibly tall on the white, and held them steady
  with the black.

### Regions, south to north

| Region | Biome | What's there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Keys** | Florida-style keys: a chain of low sand and coral islands, mangroves, turquoise shallows, long timber causeways, summer storms | Saltpenny Key (the pirate city), wrecks to dive, turtle beaches, the causeway road |
| **The Glades** | Sawgrass swamp and slow rivers; at night the water plants glow pale green | Stilt villages, giant marsh lizards, a ruined white-crystal pumping house |
| **The Amberwold** | An autumn forest of huge old trees that never fully drops its leaves; lamp-moths at dusk | Woodsfolk towns in the branches, mushroom caves, the old road north |
| **Callis and the Bright Coast** | Warm coast, terraced vineyards, river mouth | **Callis**, the great city; fishing towns; the white quarries |
| **The Sunder** | A rift valley where the white pulled the ground apart: stone spires and slabs hang in the air on their crystal, and waterfalls pour off them | The Assembly's lift-towers, skyfarms, the deepest delves |
| **The Glass Flats** | A salt desert crusted with black glass; lightning storms strike it at night | Caravan forts, salt pans, storm-hunters |
| **The Greywaste** | Dead moors, grey forests and frozen fens; nothing grows on ground where the black was dug out | Abandoned villages, the first of the Held |
| **The Held Country** | Snowfields and barrow-plains of the old northern kingdom | **Northgate**, the wall city; **Kingsbarrow**, the city of the dead (zone and raid-sized dungeon) |

### The great cities

Each is several times the size of Ansbury, built to be walked around for an hour.

- **Callis.** The Assembly city, at the river mouth on the Bright Coast. White towers twenty storeys
  high on the lift, held down by black keystones; a harbour of a hundred ships; districts for the
  guilds, the temples, the markets and the docks. The packet ship from Gullmouth comes in here.
- **Northgate.** A fortress city across the only pass into the Held Country. Its wall is three
  hundred years old and has never been needed until now. Barracks, a cathedral, and the
  gatekeepers' archive.
- **Saltpenny Key.** The Brethren's city on the outermost key, built out of and on top of wrecked
  ships: rope bridges, a market in a beached galleon's hold, taverns in hulls, the fence's vault
  in a sunk warship reached at low tide. Like ESO's Abah's Landing, but pirates.

## The Brethren of Saltpenny (faction)

- A free company of captains who answer to no Charter and no Assembly. They run anything that pays
  more when it's untaxed, including (this act's trouble) the black.
- **Standing** with the Brethren works like the region standing already in the game: smuggling runs,
  raids on Harrowgate convoys, sunk cargo to raise, duels in the Cockpit. Higher standing opens the
  fence, the shipwrights' yard, Brethren gear and the back rooms of Saltpenny.
- **Captain Ned Vey** speaks for the captains. He'd sell you anything except his crew.

## Ships

- **The packet** runs Gullmouth to Callis and back on a fixed timetable; you ride it (a sailing
  scene) the way ESO's boats work. Later packets run Callis to Saltpenny and Callis to Northgate.
- **Your own ship (endgame).** Built at the Saltpenny or Callis shipwrights from timber, ironwork,
  canvas and rope you gather and craft yourself: a long farm, by design. Three sizes (a cutter, a
  brig, a galleon). A ship is housing: furnish its cabins like a house, anchor it in any open water,
  or berth it at a coastal city's docks (Gullmouth, Callis, Saltpenny, Holme Staithe), where friends
  can visit it.

## The expansion story: The Third Daughter

Five chapters after Act III, from Nessa Crane at Gullmouth.

1. **Half a Crew** (XVII) [in game]. A Morrow ship comes into Gullmouth half-crewed, carrying passengers
   from the north who won't say what they left. While the player was at Holmsey, Harrowgate & Daughters
   sent a ship east for crystal: the *Third Daughter*, with **Mr Pryce** aboard as the company's buyer
   and Delia Harrowgate's signature on his papers. Delia wants to know what her name has been signed to.
   She pays your passage, and Joan pays too, separately, without telling her.
2. **Nine Hundred Weight** (XVIII) [in game]. The great city. The Assembly's towers have been buying black, more than they've ever
   needed, to hold down towers that have started to lift as the white in them settles. Pryce has
   been here: he sold the Assembly the black, and nobody asked where it came from.
3. **What Pays** (XIX) [in game]. The black comes up the coast in Brethren hulls. Ned Vey will tell you where they
   load it, for a price: a Harrowgate convoy, or a favour later. The Brethren didn't dig it. They
   only carry it.
4. **The Greywaste.** The black was dug out of the northern barrows. The old kingdom buried its dead
   under black so they would lie still. Take the black away and they don't: these are the **Held**.
   The villages emptied one by one, and Northgate has shut its gate.
5. **Kingsbarrow.** Pryce is in the city of the dead with his diggers, still digging, because the
   Assembly has already paid. The old king's barrow is the last and deepest seam.

**Endings** (as in the earlier acts, the choice changes the world):

- **Put it back.** The black goes back into the barrows and the Held lie down. Callis must take its
  tallest towers down a storey at a time before they lift, and the Assembly never forgives the
  Counties.
- **Hold the line.** Northgate keeps the Held behind its wall, Harrowgate sells the black on
  under Assembly licence, and the Greywaste is given up. Profitable, for some.
- **The Brethren's way.** Vey sinks the black in deep water off the Keys where nobody can dig it.
  The Held lie down slowly. The towers come down anyway, and Saltpenny is owed a favour by everyone.

In every ending Pryce comes home, but whether he sails on the *Third Daughter* or in irons
depends on what you did. Delia reads his book at last.
