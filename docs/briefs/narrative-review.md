# AAA narrative review and story overhaul

The owner's brief, word for word, as sent 2026-11-11.

EVERREALM — AAA NARRATIVE QUALITY REVIEW & STORY DEVELOPMENT OVERHAUL
I want to elevate Everrealm's existing main storyline to the storytelling standard of major BioWare and CD Projekt RED RPGs.
Reference quality:

* Dragon Age: Origins — companion relationships, moral complexity, difficult decisions.
* Mass Effect trilogy — long-term character development, continuity, consequences, and emotional payoffs.
* The Witcher 3 — grounded storytelling, morally complicated characters, unexpected consequences.
* Baldur's Gate 3 — companion personalities, reactive dialogue, interpersonal conflict, and player agency.
* Elder Scrolls Online and Guild Wars 2 — expansive regional storytelling and a world that feels larger than the player.

This is NOT a request to discard the existing storyline.
The existing ten-act, 112-chapter plan is the foundation. I want you to critically evaluate it and improve it.
1. PROTECT EXISTING CANON
Before making changes, read:

* LORE.md
* NARRATIVE_STYLE.md
* The complete main story plan.
* Existing companion definitions and dialogue.
* Existing quest implementations.
* Relevant regional lore documents.

LORE.md remains authoritative.
Preserve the existing grounded writing style. No generic fantasy prophecy, chosen-one storyline, recycled AI fantasy dialogue, or meaningless dramatic monologues.
2. CONDUCT A STORY CRITIQUE
Evaluate the complete campaign.
Identify:

* Weak character motivations.
* Plot holes and inconsistencies.
* Underdeveloped antagonists.
* Predictable twists.
* Poorly established deaths.
* Chapters that feel like filler.
* Weak transitions between acts.
* Unclear supernatural rules.
* Decisions that lack meaningful consequences.
* Characters who disappear without satisfying conclusions.

Be willing to challenge existing writing decisions.
Do not praise something simply because it already exists.
3. THE PROTAGONIST
Preserve the ordinary-stranger concept.
Do not introduce a chosen-one identity, ancient bloodline, or predetermined destiny.
However, strengthen the player's personal involvement.
The protagonist should develop relationships, influence political events, build a reputation, and become important through their decisions.
NPCs should remember the player's actions.
Create opportunities for players to establish their own personality through dialogue, decisions, and behavior.
4. THE COMPANIONS
Preserve Wenna, Ottilie, Tam, Wat, and Wend.
Deepen their personalities and relationships.
Give them:

* More spontaneous conversations.
* Inter-companion friendships and rivalries.
* Opinions about the player's decisions.
* Conflicts that don't always require player intervention.
* Personal quests with meaningful conclusions.
* Character development that evolves throughout all ten acts.
* Dialogue that reflects their relationship with the player.

Avoid making companions feel like quest-giving machines.
Review the death and departure mechanics. Make major character losses emotionally earned and properly foreshadowed.
The hidden third solution to saving Wenna and Tam must be discoverable through storytelling, not arbitrary stat requirements.
5. RECONSIDER THE MAJOR LOSSES
Review Ursel, Joan, Wenna, Tam, Wat, and the destruction of Stair.
Not every death needs to be avoidable. However, not every major tragedy should be predetermined either.
Distinguish between fixed narrative events and consequences that players can influence.
Deaths should have lasting effects on other characters, dialogue, settlements, and later chapters.
Never kill a character simply to manufacture sadness.
6. ELEVATE HILDA AND PRYCE
Hilda needs to become a genuinely complex antagonist.
Develop her history, motivations, fears, relationships, and gradual moral deterioration.
Players should understand how she became the person she is.
Pryce should remain honest but morally compromised.
Explore the difference between honesty and goodness. His willingness to conduct exploitative transactions openly should make him particularly interesting.
Avoid conventional villain behavior.
7. CONNECT THE SUPERNATURAL AND HUMAN STORYLINES
Clarify the relationship between:

* The Held.
* The Old Mother.
* The green seam.
* The black.
* The Corvo pipes.
* The economic and political systems.

Establish consistent rules and historical explanations.
The supernatural elements should reinforce the human themes rather than replace them.
Maintain the principle that the trouble has human causes.
8. MAKE THE WORLD REACTIVE
The story should affect the world.
Where technically feasible, implement:

* Changed NPC dialogue.
* Altered settlement conditions.
* Different faction relationships.
* Companion reactions.
* Changed trade and economic conditions.
* Visible consequences of major decisions.
* Different endings and epilogues.

Make consequences persist across acts.
9. EXPAND REGIONAL STORYTELLING
Every major region should have its own identity, local conflicts, memorable NPCs, and self-contained storyline.
Regional stories should contribute to the overarching campaign without feeling like disconnected side content.
In particular, develop Verandah as an important wealthy port city with its own merchant politics, military organization, surrounding forts, and maritime conflicts.
Do not force Verandah into existing canon without checking its geographical and historical compatibility.
10. PRODUCTION REQUIREMENTS
Before rewriting anything, provide:

1. A complete narrative critique.
2. A list of recommended changes.
3. A revised ten-act narrative outline.
4. A companion development and relationship matrix.
5. A character death and consequence matrix.
6. A supernatural lore and continuity reference.
7. A chapter numbering and continuity audit.

Resolve the existing chapter count discrepancies before expanding the campaign.
Preserve completed content wherever possible.
Do not break existing quest systems or discard implemented narrative material without justification.
Do not start implementing the entire overhaul immediately.
First present the revised narrative plan for review.
FINAL CREATIVE STANDARD
I want players to become emotionally attached to Everrealm.
I want them to laugh with companions, disagree with them, regret decisions, become invested in ordinary NPCs, lose people they thought would be around forever, and remember individual conversations long after completing the campaign.
I want characters who feel like real people, not fantasy archetypes.
I want decisions that players debate with friends.
I want reveals that make earlier chapters feel completely different when revisited.
I want the final chapter to make players think about the journey they experienced, not simply celebrate defeating a villain.
Everrealm should have its own recognizable narrative identity.
Do not imitate the plots or characters of the reference games. Match their standard of character writing, emotional depth, world-building, and narrative ambition.
Quality over quantity. Every chapter must earn its place.
